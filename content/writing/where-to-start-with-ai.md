The question I hear most about AI is "where do I start?" People are usually waiting for something first: the right course, the right tool, a free weekend to study.

You can skip all of that. Open the AI tool you already have, tell it what you're trying to do, and ask it to guide you. The thing you want to learn about is also the best teacher of it.

## Ask it the way you'd ask an expert

Don't try to write a clever prompt. Describe your situation the way you would to a consultant sitting across the table:

> I run a ten-person landscaping business and I lose every Sunday night to invoices. How could AI help with that, and what's the first thing I should try?

A plain, specific description of a real problem gets you a better answer than any prompt trick.

## Let it lead

When you don't know what to ask next, say so. A few lines I use all the time:

- "Ask me questions until you understand what I need."
- "Explain that as if I've never done this before."
- "What would you do next, and why?"
- "What am I missing?"

You don't have to know the vocabulary. The tool will give it to you as you go.

## The same approach works for building an agent

An agent sounds like something that needs an engineering team. Start the same way: describe the job in plain language.

1. Tell the tool what the work is, step by step, as you do it today.
2. Ask it which steps it could take over and what it would need access to.
3. Ask it to build the smallest version that does one step well.
4. Run it, see where it falls short, and tell the tool what went wrong.

That loop of describing, trying, and correcting is most of what building with AI is.

## What to do this week

Pick one recurring task that annoys you. Describe it to your AI tool and ask how it would help. Then do the first thing it suggests.

You'll learn more from that hour than from another month of reading about AI.
